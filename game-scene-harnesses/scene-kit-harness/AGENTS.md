# Scene Kit scope

Follow the repository contract. The local Studio is only a deterministic
consumer of the offline kit, compiler and command editor. The user-requested
local generation CLI may use an explicitly selected optional host adapter under
the root generation-chain exception. Keep that execution separate from Studio.
Do not add provider calls to the core, arbitrary file/command endpoints, deployment,
accounts, storage services or queues. Tests use the synthetic kit exclusively.

Browser camera, tool, selection and layer visibility belong to editor state;
they must not silently alter the author document. Imports are validated before
replacing the current document. One gesture is one atomic core command batch;
cancel, blur and lost capture must discard an uncommitted gesture.

An explicitly configured, verified terrain-study appearance may be previewed on
the existing map. Keep it labeled as a study; the author map, compiled kit source,
exports and visual acceptance remain separate. Read only the configured immutable
snapshot over HTTP, never a request-selected path. Tests use synthetic artwork.

The user-requested project-management extension follows the root exception:
bounded user-selected project/scene ZIP bytes, verified immutable asset snapshots,
multiple independent author maps and atomic saves under one startup-configured
ignored workspace. Never accept paths from HTTP, execute package contents or add
generation calls. Validate before adoption; failed imports/saves retain prior
documents and history. Empty projects have no kit and cannot compile until bound.

For environment-library generation, follow the root local exchange exception.
Keep content interpretation and image rendering independently frozen. User
inputs only reach the content interpreter. Bind the image plan to the checked
project style anchor and structured physical content. For one-click generation,
the user's submission authorizes one independently frozen content request and
one independently frozen image request, each with its own digest-bound decision
and durable reservation. Studio records decisions and verifies imports without
invoking an adapter. Only the separately started CLI may dispatch. A checked
sprite may enter its originating project automatically under this submission,
with a receipt separate from human visual acceptance.

Animation generation follows the root single-operation exchange scope. The
animation draft receives only an accepted frame sheet for its exact project,
asset and state. The browser still saves the animation settings explicitly.

New reusable terrain plans must cover the whole editable perimeter. Use
`coverageProfile: 'full-perimeter-v1'` with all seven native side/top/bottom roles
when the map exposes an underside or its brush permits arbitrary painting.
For new natural perimeter plans, select
`naturalSurfaceProfile: 'reference-defined-cover-v1'` alongside the explicit
native-size and render-envelope profiles. Surface cover belongs to the accepted
reference, checked material and role brief; do not inherit leaf/soil/grass/moss
defaults from the natural family or substitute the city profile. Historical
requests without this option retain their original behavior.
Prefer the explicit native-size options
`nativeSizeProfile: 'bounded-native-4m-v1'` and
`renderEnvelopeProfile: 'visible-alpha8-outward16-native-rgba-v1'` for new plans:
they bound longest side and total pixels while reviewing actual transparent
margins. Do not rewrite legacy 2048-bound plans after a tool ignores their size.
An eligible size-only failure may use the exact-reviewed zero-call native-size
preparation; keep its original failed QA and source bytes. Never auto-adopt it.
The measured v2 baseline
`junctionProfile: 'native-bottom-endpoint-underlap-v1'` remains available: convex lower corners
take ownership from the old side, and concave contacts extend the unchanged
native side phase only inside the bottom cutback band. Verify the upstream
opaque collar before accepting the profile. Historical kits without it retain
their original rendering.
For new full-perimeter deliveries that need natural short platforms, bridge
ceilings and narrow openings, explicitly select the additive
`junctionProfile: 'native-bottom-adaptive-coverage-v2'`. It retains the checked
endpoint underlap while fading the lower corner's upper arm and fitting the
endpoint fade to the real available span. Supported top cover comes only from
the original solid's exposed cap triangles and draws after native edges; never
fill an opening, reconstruct a silhouette or recolor pixels to hide a seam.
Keep original RGBA, native frame, collar, envelope, world UV, collision and work
budgets. This opt-in does not rewrite historical v1 plans, kits or results.
When native lower corners leave the author solid uncovered inside the bottom
cutback band, a new v2 delivery may explicitly select
`bodySupportProfile: 'author-solid-world-body-v1'` together with that adaptive
junction profile. It retains only the original author-solid body support with
its existing world UVs inside the bounded exposed-bottom region. Original
native Alpha, exterior silhouette, true holes/air and collision stay separate;
never expand that support outside author occupancy or fabricate corner paint.
Keep the old absent-field behavior and source/geometry budgets unchanged.
An existing verified four-source side kit may instead add the three native
bottom roles through the checked upgrade. Preserve historical four-source plans;
do not call them complete-perimeter deliveries or rotate their top lips to fill
missing bottom roles. Measure joins, collars, repeat windows and scale from each
actual source family, never copy another sample's pixel coordinates. Missing or
incompatible artwork remains a visible capability gap until a fresh exact-plan
generation and independent review succeed. Fixed whole-platform skins stay
isolated unless the user explicitly invokes their undoable conversion to painted
terrain; that operation replaces their appearance while retaining the cells.

For each new material calibration, distinguish the thin designed surface cover,
its attached rock shoulder and the full sampled cap window. Measured `capDepth`
may include that whole transition; it is not evidence of cover thickness. Select
the existing full-cap or `world-body-native-lip-v1` interface from the reference
and actual source, and measure any native lip end/fade rows. Never impose a
global row count, thin lip or material substance, or compress the complete cap
UV depth to hide a broad overlay. Preserve original sources and historical
controls. When selecting `materialLoop`, inspect broad rock-form progression
across a complete repeat period and the joins within it; equal first/last rows
or zero row-L1 error alone do not establish visual continuity. Moving the body
origin only moves a seam. Validate the full terrain combination matrix and
actual Studio separately before claiming a resolved interface or repeat.

An accepted reference may contain explicitly reviewed framing crops, while its
new transparent sources must still be complete. For such decorative groups,
the default observed-envelope contain is not a final scale calibration. Measure
visible features and contact points from the new sources, bind explicit
composition transforms and retain native density sampling. Keep unseen bases
and supports labeled as design inference; do not certify grounding or restore
hidden geometry from an observed envelope alone.

For new continuous backdrop/valley bands that must tolerate camera movement,
explicitly select `background-band-v1` instead of applying the isolated object's
closed-silhouette/12-percent art-margin instruction. Keep v4 content ownership,
the natural transparent upper contour and the original v27 transparent border
gate. Bind a finite camera domain, actual layer parallax, camera modes and three
side/bottom continuation edges; this version does not certify occlusion or
tiling. Require full-native-frame calibration before deriving a band, never the
old observed-envelope contain or an ordinary transform bypass. Source Alpha
bounds establish a range condition only; content continuity and art still need
independent review. Old scopes, frozen requests and author projects stay intact.

When a new background-band request needs an explicit coverage example, select
`background-band-guided-v2` and its fixed `three-edge-continuation-v1` guide.
Input 1 remains the complete accepted art reference; input 2 is the canonical
role-specific neutral coverage raster, bound by its recipe and PNG digest.
Require a two-image-capable adapter. Never substitute failed artwork, an arbitrary
guide or an opaque display surround for transparent output. New hints and inputs
require fresh exact-plan confirmation; v1 requests remain byte-identical.

For new sources whose finite side/bottom cuts are intentional, select the additive
`background-band-coverage-v3` with `natural-top-finite-three-cuts-v1` explicitly.
Its normalized coverage guide uses the band's actual canvas aspect and a soft
relative feature budget. Only this new profile permits paint at left, right and
bottom PNG cuts; the natural top still needs the original transparent-border gate
and real transparent sky. Old v1/v2 requests and rejected QA remain unchanged.
Finite-cut technical success does not prove camera coverage or usable feature
scale. Preserve all native RGBA and require full-frame placement plus independent
same-layer, scale and continuous-coverage review before delivery.

For new open-depth bands whose material forms have real water/air gaps between
them, explicitly select `background-band-open-depth-v4` and its fixed
`open-depth-three-cut-coverage-v1` transparent example. Painted forms continue
through a side/bottom finite cut where they cross it; true empty space can also
continue through those cuts. Do not inherit the v3 solid-body or opaque surround
example, fill voids with another layer's water/sky, or repair openings after
generation. Keep the natural-top Alpha gate, actual native frame, finite camera
domain and exact guide replay. Bounding ranges do not certify topology or art;
old v3 plans, guide bytes, failed sources and QA remain unchanged.

For a new valley whose broad side-connected banks should leave an open trench,
explicitly select the additive band-v4 guidance subtype
`open-trench-three-cut-coverage-v2`. The fixed neutral example has broad stepped
side bodies and a through-trench empty route, without enclosed holes or isolated
thin posts. This subtype is valley-only, not an automatic choice for backdrops,
city groups or every valley. It guides asset-local topology rather than full-scene
coordinates, subject counts or palette. Preserve the original open-depth-v1
guide/request bytes and all technical gates; new topology does not certify model
compliance or allow old failed sources to be adopted without a new decision.

For new source briefs with separate distant-backdrop and rear-depth groups,
state both sides of their ownership boundary explicitly. Exclude the named
neighboring depth content rather than only "near" or "independent" objects;
an attached distant forest may still belong to the separately planned valley.
Do not globally ban vegetation when it is part of the designed backdrop, or
remove a valley's own snow/rock walls. Apply new wording through a fresh plan,
never edit a frozen request or transfer an unused authorization to it.

For separate foreground groups, define the primary mass and the permitted
extent of secondary attachments on both sides of the ownership boundary.
A rock group with a few attached dead twigs must not silently grow into the
separately planned fallen-root group. Exclude that complete neighboring form,
not every small local branch or every type of vegetation. Keep approximate
identity and overall composition separate from exact-count or pixel matching.
Apply such corrections through new source scopes and fresh exact-plan approval;
never crop away or recolor a wrong generated group to call its scope pure.
For new landmark, scenery and foreground scopes, explicitly select
`groundingProfile: 'scope-owned-support-v1'` through the versioned scope helper.
This preserves the `isolated-layer-v4` intent while making generic support
instructions subordinate to the single-asset brief. Do not silently retrofit
historical plans or turn legitimate brief-owned roots into a global exclusion.

For new source requests, explicitly select `source-owned-appearance-v1` through
the source-appearance helper. Freeze source-local appearance rules rather than
copying or heuristically filtering complete scene style rules or free-form shared
observations. Keep the scene style name, original reference and visual-coherence
evidence; only generic role coherence instructions and the explicitly selected
source appearance rules enter this new prompt branch. Content, counts, support
and coverage stay owned by their typed source contracts. Old scopes, requests and
failed QA remain unchanged; replacing an unsubmitted request still needs its own
new exact-plan authorization. This isolates instruction ownership and never
guarantees model compliance or replaces source and actual-Studio review.

Before judging a suspected halo, painted surround or filled opening in a
transparent raw PNG, use the public source-alpha-review CLI to inspect true
continuous-Alpha source-over views on its fixed white, dark and checker
backgrounds. Raw Alpha-zero pixels may retain hidden RGB that a previewer
incorrectly displays. Prefer the existing plan/result/QA binding; an explicit
local-PNG review stays unbound. Keep every original source byte and QA verdict.
Nonzero-Alpha paint still contributes, including soft Alpha and sources without
any fully opaque pixels. These opaque diagnostic images never clean or replace
the source, repair a silhouette, authorize compute, or grant technical/art
acceptance. Use a fresh output outside the workflow; add no HTTP route.

Coherent source canvas padding requires explicit review.
Keep the original border-v1 and canvas-gutter-v1 qualifications unchanged.
The separate `low-alpha-margin-v2` profile may prepare a rejected v27 transparent
source only when replayed QA has exactly `COHERENT_CLIPPED_SILHOUETTE`, its outer
two-pixel border maximum Alpha is greater than 1 and at most 7, and its nonempty
Alpha-at-least-8 bounds have at least two pixels of margin on every side.
Retain every original RGBA value and failed QA, bind the exact preview/proposal
acceptance, and preserve the other unused exact-plan authorizations. Padding
adds zero image calls and cannot establish missing contour completeness or art
acceptance. Never silently select this profile after another gate fails.

Native terrain perimeter v35/v36/v37 sources have a separate reviewed
`native-terrain-edge-transparent-canvas-2px-v1` preparation. It only accepts the
replayed sole `TERRAIN_EDGE_NATIVE_GUTTER` failure with the bounded weak border
and visible-body margins, within the same native size and pixel limits. Do not
reuse the coherent or size-only entry points for it. Preserve original RGBA,
the failed QA and the exact preview acceptance. Its consumer nativeFrame excludes
only the newly added zero canvas; controls, world size and the 256-world budget
retain the original pixel frame. Preparation adds no image calls or authorizations;
only the unchanged, unused requests may continue after explicit exact adoption.

New coherent native perimeter plans use `alpha-one-gutter-warning-v1`. When
the outer two-pixel band has maximum Alpha 1 and the image has an Alpha >= 8
body, classify the touched outer edge as a technical warning. Pass the original
PNG through with its original frame when the other PNG gates pass. Keep
measured joins and independent human art acceptance.

For historical perimeter plans with a strict transparent gutter, the operator
may enable `alpha-one-transparent-canvas-2px-v1` to complete the checked
zero-call preparation for a full seven-role batch. It requires the sole gutter failure,
an outer two-pixel band with maximum Alpha 1, the original minimum visible-body
margins, and passing padded PNG QA and size budgets. Record the automatic policy
and preserve the original failure, all RGBA and the original native frame.
Keep human art acceptance independent and continue only unused authorized
requests. Other failures and unknown attempts still stop the batch.
